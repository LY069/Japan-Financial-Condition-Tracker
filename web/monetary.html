Definitions

What the two indices measure

Both are polarity-signed z-scores against each indicator's longest available history, so a positive reading always means easier money or more liquid markets.

Monetary conditions

The stance and quantity of money — how cheap and how plentiful money is relative to history.

  • Real short ratesEx-post real policy rate and real 1-year rate (nominal minus realized core CPI): the price of money.
  • Exchange rateReal effective yen (BIS broad, 2020=100) and USD/JPY — the classic MCI channel; a weaker yen is easier.
  • Money & creditM2, M3 and bank-lending growth: the quantity of money and credit, the "money view" of inflation.
  • BoJ balance sheetGrowth of total BoJ assets — QQE expansion versus the post-2024 taper and contraction.

Liquidity conditions

The availability of funds and the ease of trading, tracked in three layers from the central bank outward.

  • Central-bank liquidityBase money and BoJ current-account balances (reserves) — the raw supply of settlement money.
  • Funding liquidityEase of bank funding: TONA versus the policy target, 3m TIBOR–OIS, GC repo conditions and the USD/JPY cross-currency basis.
  • Market liquidityEase of trading: JGB market functioning DI, bid-ask spreads, free float (BoJ share of JGBs) and equity volatility.
How this differs from financial conditions. The Financial Conditions Tracker follows the BoJ two-stage framework — the outcomes of transmission (yield curve and real rates by maturity, funding costs and availability, asset prices, funding volumes). This page tracks the inputs upstream of that channel: the stance and quantity of money the BoJ supplies, and how freely it circulates through funding and securities markets.
Monetary conditions

Stance and quantity of money

Four axes: real short-rate stance, money & credit aggregates, the exchange rate and the BoJ balance-sheet stance. Equal-weight mean of the four axis scores.

— —

Money & credit aggregates

% y/y

M2, M3 and bank lending outstanding, year-on-year. Slow money growth with firm lending points to a sluggish money multiplier rather than weak credit demand.

Exchange rate

REER (left) · USD/JPY (right)

Real effective yen (BIS broad, 2020=100) on the left axis; USD/JPY on the right. A lower REER / higher USD/JPY is a weaker yen — easier on the MCI convention.

BoJ balance sheet

% y/y

Total assets and the monetary base. Negative growth marks the post-QQE taper: reserves are being drained as JGB reinvestment is reduced.

Real short-rate stance

%, ex-post

Real policy rate (2004+) and real 1-year rate (1974+), nominal minus realized core CPI. Below zero = negative real cost of money. Series are aligned by date.

Liquidity conditions

Availability of funds and ease of trading

Three layers: central-bank liquidity, funding liquidity and market liquidity. Equal-weight mean of the three axis scores.

— —

Funding liquidity

spreads in pp (left) · USD/JPY basis in bp (right)

3m TIBOR–OIS and the overnight call rate minus the policy target on the left axis; the 3-month USD/JPY cross-currency basis on the right. A more negative basis means dearer dollar funding for Japanese banks.

JGB market functioning

DI (bars, left) · BoJ share % and 10s30s slope pp (right)

BoJ Bond Market Survey functioning DI (quarterly, 2015+; negative = impaired) with the BoJ's share of outstanding JGBs and the 30Y–10Y curve slope, a super-long term-premium gauge. A falling share restores free float; a steeper 10s30s signals term-premium stress.

Bid-ask & volatility

10y JGB bid-ask (left) · Nikkei VI % and 10Y realized vol bp (right)

Normalized 10-year JGB bid-ask spread, the Nikkei Volatility Index and realized 10Y JGB volatility (12-month standard deviation of monthly yield changes). Wider spreads and higher volatility both mean thinner market liquidity.

Term-premium stress from the JGB curve

computed from MoF yields · realized 10Y vol (bp) & 10s30s slope (pp)

Realized volatility is the 12-month standard deviation of monthly 10Y yield changes; the 10s30s slope proxies super-long term premium / fiscal-risk stress. Both are live (MoF data) and enter the market-liquidity axis with polarity −1 (higher = less liquid).

Composite indices — history

MCI (monetary), LCI (liquidity) and the combined MLCI. Positive = easier / more liquid than history; negative = tighter / scarcer. MCI extends back to 1974 via the real-rate axis; LCI and MLCI begin in 2005.

Implications

What the readings mean

How monetary and liquidity conditions feed through to growth, inflation and the conduct of policy.

Growth

Demand and credit

  • Money & credit growth and real rates drive nominal demand: subdued M2/M3 growth with real rates rising toward zero is a headwind for spending, even if bank lending remains firm.
  • Weak REER supports export competitiveness and tourism receipts, but the import-price squeeze on real household income offsets part of the gain.
  • Funding stress (wider TIBOR–OIS, more negative USD/JPY basis) would raise bank funding costs and curb lending — the channel that turns a liquidity shock into a growth shock.
Inflation

The money view and the yen

  • Money view: slow money growth and a less-negative real policy rate point to diminishing monetary impetus to inflation over the medium term.
  • Weak REER lifts import prices and near-term headline inflation — the exchange-rate channel is still the easiest part of the monetary mix.
  • Balance-sheet contraction and reserve drain tighten liquidity and lift term premia, adding to long-yield pressure independent of the policy rate.
Monetary-policy conduct

Rate control and QT

  • JGB market functioning is the binding constraint on the pace of balance-sheet normalization (QT): a recovering DI and a falling BoJ share of JGBs give room to keep tapering purchases.
  • Rate control: the overnight call rate trading close to target with ample current-account balances means the interest-on-reserves floor is still operative despite the reserve drain.
  • Financial stability: orderly TIBOR–OIS and basis readings keep the bank-lending channel open; a sharp widening would argue for slower QT or dollar liquidity backstops before further rate hikes.

Methodology

—

≥ +0.15 easy / liquid −0.15 … +0.15 broadly neutral < −0.15 tight / scarce

Direction (▲ easing / ▼ tightening / → stable) is the trailing 6-month change in each score — whether a signal is turning easier or tighter, shown alongside its level.

Indices

MCI — Monetary Conditions Index: real short rates, money & credit, exchange rate, BoJ balance sheet. LCI — Liquidity Conditions Index: central-bank, funding and market liquidity. MLCI — combined index, the mean of MCI and LCI.

Indicator weights & assumptions

Each axis score is a weight-weighted mean of its member scores; MCI and LCI are equal-weight means of their axes and MLCI averages the two. Core gauges carry full weight (1.0); secondary or overlapping gauges (M3, USD/JPY, bid-ask, BoJ share, equity volatility, dollar basis) are down-weighted (0.5–0.75). Polarity orients each series so a higher score always means easier money or more ample liquidity.
